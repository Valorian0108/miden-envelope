Every element, with an ID.

This is the full contents of the preview, broken down by where each piece sits. Nothing here is left unnamed, so you can point at an ID and say that one.

The preview is a generic build. This page exists so it can be replaced, piece by piece, with a design that isn't.

What the thing depicts, in plain words

Someone pays you. On a public network the payment leaves a trace even when the amount is hidden. Miden seals the inside of a private note — the money, the asset, who it's for. But the note still has to be put somewhere the network can find it, so it gets a label on the outside. That label is public, and by default it is worked out from the receiver's account number.

Account numbers don't change. So every payment to the same person gets the same label. Anyone watching can collect the labels and say: these six payments belong to one person. They still don't know who. But they know it's a who — and that is already a history.

This shows a way to break that up. Each payment gets a different label, picked at random from a small pool of labels that all lead back to the same receiver. The receiver listens on the whole pool, so it still finds its money. A watcher sees six different labels and has nothing to group together. The dial is how big the pool is — a wide pool means more labels to hide among but more work for the receiver; a narrow pool is the reverse.

Then it shows the two ways that is weaker than it sounds. An observer holding the public list of account numbers doesn't need labels to repeat — it works backwards from any label to a shortlist of a few possible accounts, and guesses. And an observer who already knows your account number isn't guessing at all: it registers exactly your pool and catches every payment, whatever the dial says.

So it isn't about being invisible. It's about being one face in a crowd — and how big the crowd really is.

How the IDs work

CAST-02.e — region, then number, then a part. CAST is where it lives, 02 is which element, .e is which piece of it. Parts are lettered the same way across repeating elements, so CAST-01.e and CAST-03.e are the same slot on different characters.

Freedom column

free — yours entirely, restyle or replace. content-bound — the shape is yours, the value is data and cannot change. structural — cannot be removed or merged, because it carries the argument.

Appears column

b1–b6 meaning beat 1 through beat 6. The page is scrolled, not clicked: each beat is a full screen of scrolling text and the scene above stays put.

Nine positions is a design decision, not a fact. The underlying thing is a pool width from 7 to 14. If your design wants a continuous fader, five stops, or two extremes only, that is legitimate — but the page then loses the ability to show where the shortlist collapses, which is the most interesting part of the dial.

What your design has to decide

OPEN

Everything above is a slot. These are the choices that sit on top of them, and they are yours. I've written my current answer next to each so you can overrule it rather than start from nothing.

  • What are the watchers, physically? Currently three faces — a round blob, a square with glasses, a fox. CAST-0n.b is a free slot: person, animal, machine, object, abstract glyph. The only constraint is that CAST-03.b must have exactly one expression.
  • Is the whole thing one screen or a scroll? Currently six full screens of scrolling. A single-screen version would need the six cards as a side panel or a sequence you click through, and would lose the slow reveal of BEAT-04 and BEAT-05.
  • Where does the dial live, and what is it? Currently a knob bottom-left. It could be a lever, a fader, a row of stops, or a physical object in the scene. Nine positions is my choice, not a law.
  • Is a payment an envelope? Currently yes. It could be a parcel, a coin, a card, a light. The only thing that must survive is that something visible on the outside names the group.
  • Three colours, one each? Orange / teal / pink right now. The third's colour should be the one that reads as unbothered, because it never changes.
  • How loud is the type? The numbers are currently monospace so they read as measurements rather than slogans. Your call.
  • Does the third character appear early? Currently asleep and dimmed until beat six, then spotlit. Showing it from the start, unmoving, is a legitimate and colder alternative.
  • Small screens? Below about 1240px the card has to slide over the scene. If your design is meant for a phone, the whole arrangement probably needs to become one card at a time.
